# Credits and licences

Moonlight XR for Frame is licensed under the GNU General Public License v3 (`LICENSE.txt`).

Built by [simplysanatori](https://github.com/simplysanatori), who also made the Steam Frame fork of Moonlight XR that this client follows.

It is based on [Moonlight XR](https://github.com/Gilleece/moonlight-android-xr) by Sean Gilleece (GPLv3). The layout, comfort, depth, audio and interface modules come from there; this project ports them from Java/GLES to C/Vulkan and replaces the Android parts (decoder, input, windowing). The viewing rooms are his too (Copyright (c) 2026 Sean Gilleece / Woodford XR, CC BY 4.0), and so is the logo the app's icon and Steam art are made from.

Moonlight XR is itself a fork of [Moonlight for Android](https://github.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-android), and Moonlight is the work of the Moonlight Game Streaming Project ([moonlight-stream](https://github.com/moonlight-stream)): Cameron Gutman, Diego Waxemberg, Aaron Neyer, Andrew Hennessy and the other contributors. The streaming protocol is [moonlight-common-c](https://github.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-common-c), and the way the client talks to Sunshine and Apollo follows [moonlight-qt](https://github.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-qt) by Cameron Gutman and contributors. The flat launcher is moonlight-qt 6.1.0 with a few patches.

The headset's menus are translated with Moonlight XR's translations (`ui/ui_strings_i18n.inc`, made from its `strings.xml` files by `tools/ui/gen_strings.py`). In Moonlight XR's words, its French and Chinese were started by [moi952](https://github.com/moi952) and [TianQuanDiWen](https://github.com/TianQuanDiWen) and the rest of the VR side was translated in house; the strings it shares with Moonlight for Android (the PC and app lists) are the Moonlight translators' work on [Weblate](https://hosted.weblate.org/projects/moonlight/moonlight-android/).

Thanks as well to the Sunshine and Apollo projects, Khronos, the LiteRT/TensorFlow Lite team, Fabio Tosi (ZipDepth), Intel ISL (MiDaS), and Bill Gardner and Keith Martin of the MIT Media Lab (KEMAR).

Every third-party component and asset is listed below. The same table is in `assets/licenses/README.md`; the licence texts it names are in the repository and in the installed `assets/` directory.

The gun model: This work is based on "G17 Gen 5 Handgun + Olight - NSW Police Force" (https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/g17-gen-5-handgun-olight-nsw-police-force-0200c248eba14385ad2bee8d4dc4e586) by 8sianDude (https://sketchfab.com/haoliu95) licensed under CC-BY-4.0 (http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Changed by this project (`tools/xr/make_gun.py`): the two spare magazines and the cartridges inside the inserted one left out; turned, scaled to a real G17's size and placed at the controller's aim pose; the textures baked into 16 flat colours (the light's lens made pale); the trigger made a separate moving part; converted to the MXFM model format; mirrored for the left hand. Its triangles are otherwise as made (29,150 of 73,200 kept, none simplified).

## How this was made

I had been using Moonlight to stream my PC to the Frame and wanted something like Virtual Desktop's VR environment for it, so I built a native version of Moonlight XR for the Frame. It started as a personal project for my own headset. It ended up good enough to share.

This is vibe coded, with manual review throughout. I wrote it with Claude Code, using Anthropic's Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5 models running on the Frame itself. I chose what to build, reviewed the code and the scripts as they were written, tested each build in the headset and reported what I saw. If that is not for you, no worries. It is more than functional, and it is a starting point for anyone who wants Moonlight in a VR environment on the Frame.

It is heavily based on Moonlight XR by Gilleece. The rooms, the 2D to 3D depth mode, the menus and the virtual surround are ports of that work. The difference is that it runs natively on SteamOS instead of through Lepton. It is GPL, so take pieces for Moonlight XR or anything else.

Next steps are manual optimisation and possibly hand tracking, depending on how far the community efforts get. Developer notes are in docs/dev.
